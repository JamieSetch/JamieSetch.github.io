My research asks how the brain's way of computing can make AI and robots more capable and easier to trust. I work across spiking neural networks, brain–computer interfaces (EEG and EMG), reinforcement learning, and the tooling that lets scientists build and test biologically faithful models.

Interests

  • Spiking neural networks and neuromorphic control for humanoid robots
  • Brain–computer interfaces and real-time neural decoding
  • Reinforcement learning, biological and artificial
  • AI safety

Experience

Reinforcement learning for biological movement

At the Max Planck Institute I applied reinforcement learning models (DQN, SARSA, Actor-Critic) to simulate optimal biological movement in three-dimensional fluid environments. I analysed large simulation datasets to find patterns and improve model reliability, and wrote up the findings for the Neural Computation Group.
